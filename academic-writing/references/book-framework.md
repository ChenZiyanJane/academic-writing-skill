# Book Framework

## Source basis

This skill is derived from the process model in *Successful Writing: Student Book*, 2nd edition (2013), edited by Zou Shen. The book is aimed at undergraduate English majors and uses graduation theses and research papers as its central writing context.

Do not reproduce long passages from the book. Use its concepts, distinctions, checklists, and workflow in paraphrased form.

## Nine-unit map

1. **Understanding Research**
   - fundamentals and features of research;
   - theoretical vs. applied research;
   - primary vs. secondary research;
   - asking research questions;
   - focusing on a topic;
   - research-paper format, research approaches, and referencing styles.

2. **Choosing a Research Topic**
   - criteria: interest, relevance, significance, practicality;
   - research context: readers, purpose, time, money/resources;
   - generating candidate topics;
   - preliminary research;
   - narrowing or broadening;
   - converting a topic into a research question.

3. **Conducting Preliminary Research I: Library Research**
   - library and reference materials;
   - search and catalog use;
   - evaluating books and internet materials;
   - documenting sources;
   - MLA and APA examples.

4. **Conducting Preliminary Research II: Field Research**
   - observation;
   - questionnaire;
   - interview;
   - descriptive statistics and basic handling of open- and closed-ended responses.

5. **Writing a Research Proposal**
   - purpose of a proposal;
   - title, introduction, literature review, methods, preliminary outline, timetable, bibliography;
   - literature-review organization and evaluation;
   - sample proposal.

6. **Writing a Working Outline**
   - purpose and necessity of outlines;
   - Roman-numeral and decimal formats;
   - topic vs. sentence outlines;
   - construction principles: parallelism, coordination, subordination, division.

7. **Composing the Draft**
   - purpose of drafting;
   - abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusion;
   - integrating information through quotation and paraphrase;
   - avoiding plagiarism.

8. **Finalizing the Paper**
   - documentation;
   - revision of content and organization;
   - editing language and mechanics;
   - supplementary parts such as cover page, contents, acknowledgements, tables/figures, abbreviations, appendices.

9. **Evaluating Sample Papers**
   - apply the preceding criteria to complete papers;
   - evaluate title, abstract, introduction, literature review, methods, results, discussion, conclusion, and documentation;
   - sample papers expose both successful and weak structural choices.

## Governing idea

The book treats academic writing as a staged process:
research orientation -> topic and question -> preliminary research -> proposal -> working outline -> draft -> source integration -> revision/editing -> evaluation.

The skill should preserve this progression. It should not reduce academic writing to vocabulary substitution or surface-level polishing.

## Scope and limitations

- Many examples come from English-language, linguistics, literature, translation, and language-teaching research.
- The source is from 2013. Web-search interfaces and citation examples are historically situated.
- Use the book for writing logic and research workflow. For current citation formatting, institutional policies, or current research tools, use the user's current requirements.
- The book introduces basic descriptive statistics but is not a full statistics or research-methods textbook. Do not extend simple examples into advanced inferential claims without additional methodological support.
